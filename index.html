How to write it

One bar per line: the chord, a colon, then one entry for each 8th note (8 entries is one 4/4 bar).

  • 5-3 means string 5, fret 3. In tab, the top line is string 1 (high e) and the bottom line is string 6 (low E).
  • 5-3+1-0 plays two strings together.
  • 4-x is an × in tab. In 弹唱 tabs (a chord name over each bar) it means “play this string with the chord shape”; in some fingerstyle tabs it means a muted hit. Choose which with “× in tab” in the practice controls.
  • 2-3~ or 2-x~ continues the previous sound on that string without plucking again. Keep its time value; + joins simultaneous strings.
  • Add @1/6 to a note for a sixth of a beat, e.g. 2-3@1/6 2-5h@1/6 2-3p@1/6 2-1@1/2. This plays a sixteenth-note triplet followed by an eighth note; h is a hammer-on and p a pull-off.
  • C:: … with two colons writes the bar in 16th notes (16 entries for a 4/4 bar), for tabs with double beams.
  • . is a rest or a held note. Two colons (C::) give each token a 16th note, three (C:::) a 32nd. D and U are strums down and up. R is a rolled strum (the wavy arrow): the strings sound one after another from the bass up. Add the strings an arrow covers to strum only those: D5-1 from string 5 to 1, U3-5 back up from 3 to 5, R5-1 rolled.
  • Em7=022030: … gives the chord its own fingering (low E string first, x = not played). Scanned sheets fill this in from their chord diagrams, and × notes then follow it.
  • [Verse] on its own line adds a section label.
  • Use h for hammer-on, p for pull-off, and s for slide on the target note, e.g. 4-3 4-5s. Scans preserve the technique; slides glide between pitches.
  • [Repeat start], [Repeat end], [Ending 1] and [Ending 2] preserve repeats. Append all pages in order, or practise them together in a collection, to follow repeats across pages.

When you hit the right string while holding the right chord key, you hear the exact fret from your tab.
